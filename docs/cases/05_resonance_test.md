---

The Aldgate Conservatory occupies a converted Victorian warehouse in East London. The conversion was done cheaply in 1962 and the building has been cold ever since. The practice wing basement has a broken handrail on its main staircase. This has been reported three times in the maintenance log — November 1972, March 1973, September 1974. Each report was acknowledged. None was acted on.

The examining panel for the winter term composition examination arrives on Monday. There are four examiners. One of them is Professor Daniel Ashby, sixty-one, a composer of modest reputation and a visiting examiner of considerable experience. He has examined at the Aldgate Conservatory twice before. He has never met Marta Solís before Monday morning. By Monday afternoon he knows exactly who she is.

---

## The Composition

In 1951, Ashby supervised a student at the Royal Academy named — at the time — Elena Varga. She was Hungarian, twenty-two, and had written a string quartet of unusual quality during her second year. Ashby recognised something in it. He helped her develop it for two years. In 1953, shortly before her graduation, Elena Varga disappeared. She had no family in England. The political situation in Hungary made inquiry difficult. The Academy recorded her as having returned home. The quartet was never performed.

In 1955, a string quartet appeared in the catalogue of a small Austrian publisher under Ashby's name. It was performed twice in Vienna, once in Paris, to moderate reviews. Ashby received a commission fee. He spent it.

The quartet was Elena Varga's. He had a copy of her manuscript. He had access to the publisher. He had the opportunity and he took it, quietly, in the way that institutional power allows certain people to take things quietly.

Elena Varga did not return to Hungary. She survived. She rebuilt herself slowly under a different name. She became Marta Solís. She has been a junior tutor at the Aldgate Conservatory for four years, teaching harmony and counterpoint to first-year students, living quietly, not composing — or so everyone believes. She has one composition to her name. She wrote it in 1951. She is watching it being examined this week under another man's name.

---

## Ashby

He recognises the quartet on Monday from the printed score alone. He has lived with it for twenty years. He knows every modulation. He sits through the student performance — a competent but not exceptional reading by a third-year ensemble — and says nothing during the session. He requests a copy of the score to review overnight.

He intends to raise it at the full panel meeting on Friday. He has spent twenty years telling himself that what he did was a kind of preservation — the work survived, was heard, reached the world, even if under wrong attribution. On Monday afternoon, sitting in the practice wing with Elena Varga's score in his hands and the knowledge that she is alive and in this building, this story stops being convincing to him.

He is found at the bottom of the practice wing basement stairs on Thursday evening. The lights are off. The handrail is unsecured. He went down alone after nine pm.

---

## Marta

She switched off the stairwell lights from the ground floor panel at nine-fifteen. She knew his habit of working late in the basement practice rooms — she had watched it for four years, knowing only that he examined here occasionally, not knowing it would matter until this week. She did not touch him. She did not need to. She knew the handrail. She knew the lights. She knew his schedule. She went home at nine-thirty and was seen by her neighbour at nine forty-five.

What she did not know is that the original manuscript — her manuscript, with her name in her own handwriting on the first page — exists in a single copy in the archive of the Royal Academy of Music, filed under uncollected student work from 1951–1953, accessible to any researcher who knows to ask for it.

The archive token is the case's pivot. It surfaces late, LATE phase, and when it does it shifts the `motive_dim` from simple self-protection to something more complex: Marta killed not just to protect her secret but because the secret was already partially exposed regardless. The archive means she could not have kept it forever. She killed to buy time for a freedom she was already losing.

---

## The Student

James Okafor is twenty-two and his scholarship depends on this week's examination result. He argued with Ashby at lunch on Wednesday about the panel's methodology — a public, witnessed argument that three people remember clearly. His scholarship decision was due Friday. He is the case's most visible red herring and also its most sympathetic figure. He had nothing to do with Ashby's death. He will spend the rest of his life knowing he argued with the man hours before he died.

---

## Design notes

This is rated easy because the mechanism does not support Okafor at all — he had no access to the maintenance log, no reason to know about the handrail, no way to know Ashby's evening schedule with the specificity required. The redirect from Okafor to Marta happens within the first five or six turns for most players.

The difficulty lies elsewhere: in assembling the composition history. The 1951 manuscript, the 1953 disappearance, the 1955 publication, the 1924 death of the forger Claes — wait, Claes is Case 02. This case's composition history is: 1951 quartet → 1953 disappearance → 1955 Austrian publication. These are three distinct EARLY/MID/LATE tokens that must be placed in sequence to reconstruct the stolen work. The player builds the story of the theft as they build the case.

The archive token recontextualises the motive. Before it surfaces, Marta's motive is "protect the secret." After it surfaces, the motive is "the secret was already going to surface — she killed to buy a few more years of the life she had built." This is a harder, sadder truth than self-preservation. It is what makes this an easy case mechanically but not emotionally.

Marta's alibi — home by nine-thirty, neighbour sees her at nine forty-five — is genuine and is never broken. She did not push him. The mechanism is the physical environment of the building and her knowledge of it. The case closes on `suspect:marta_solis`, `event:stairwell_fall`, `motive:stolen_composition`, all correct.
