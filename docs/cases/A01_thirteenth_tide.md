# Adventure Case A01 — The Thirteenth Tide
## Caribbean · Nassau & Beyond · 1697 · One Year Before The Dead Calm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Case ID** | `thirteenth_tide` |
| **Type** | Adventure (sequential basins, chosen state) |
| **Difficulty** | medium |
| **Basins** | 4 |
| **Protagonist** | Sera Vane, 23, cartographer's daughter |
| **Final triad** | `object:survey_notes`, `object:castillo_document`, `event:tomas_negotiation` |
| **Key branch** | Basin 2: sell the chart fragment vs. trade it for Castillo's information |
| **Full resolution** | Archive opens; Sera decides what to do with it (game ends on the question) |

*Adventure cases converge toward a chosen state rather than a fixed truth. The field does not judge the player's choices. It records them. The world the player arrives at reflects who they chose to be while getting there.*

---

Sera Vane is twenty-three and owns three things: a hand-drawn chart showing the western half of an unnamed island, a debt of forty Spanish dollars to a tavern keeper in Nassau who has begun making specific threats about collection, and the absolute certainty — inherited from her dead father, a cartographer who spent the last twelve years of his life on this obsession — that the thing buried on the island will solve every problem she currently has and several she hasn't encountered yet.

Her father called it the Thirteenth Tide Cache. He believed a Portuguese navigator named Álvaro Dias buried a navigation archive on the island in 1541 — not treasure in the gold sense but something more valuable to the right people: accurate charts of the entire Pacific coastline of the Americas, surveyed during a voyage that was officially never completed, by a man who officially never returned. The charts would be worth a fortune to any trading company with Pacific ambitions. There are several such companies in 1697 and they are not gentle about competition.

Sera has the western half of the chart. Someone else has the eastern half. She needs a ship, a crew, the other half, and to survive the next few weeks.

---

## Basin One — Get The Ship

Sera is in Nassau with no money and no vessel. The basin opens when she assembles three enabling conditions simultaneously: transport, a reason for a crew to follow her, and resolution of the debt — because Pott the tavern keeper has a brother who is a customs official, and a woman wanted for debt in Nassau cannot sail freely from its harbour.

The transport chain: a ferryman knows about an impounded brigantine in the eastern anchorage whose owner died three months ago and whose legal status is genuinely unclear. Acquiring it requires: the harbour master's clerk has a gambling debt; the debt connects to a money lender; the money lender has been trying to acquire Pott's tavern for two years and will forgive Sera's debt in exchange for a bill of lading from the impounded ship's cargo that proves Pott received stolen goods in 1694 and has no standing to press legal claims.

The crew assembles through a separate chain: a carpenter named Mole (too old for regular employment), a cook dismissed from a merchant vessel for reasons she does not discuss, and a young gunner who owes Sera's father a debt he cannot repay in money. Each has their own enabling condition. The crew is the basin's emotional core — by the time the ship is crewed the player knows enough about each person to understand what they are risking.

---

## Basin Two — Find The Other Half

The eastern half of the chart is in the possession of a Flemish cartographer named Van der Hoek who lives in a fortified house on Nassau's eastern edge. He acquired the fragment from Sera's father in a transaction that Sera believes was theft and Van der Hoek believes was a legitimate purchase. Both are partially correct.

Van der Hoek has the interior and cache location. Sera has the coordinates and approach. Neither half is useful alone. Neither will simply trade.

Direct acquisition is impossible — his house is genuinely fortified and stealing in Nassau would make Sera a criminal in a port she needs to leave from freely. The solution requires three chains running simultaneously:

1. Van der Hoek owes a significant sum to a Dutch trading company whose representative arrives in two weeks. His debt problem creates leverage.
2. A retired Spanish pilot named Castillo possesses information about the 1541 voyage that Van der Hoek has been trying to acquire for years and would trade the fragment for without hesitation.
3. Locating Castillo — he is not in Nassau but on a small island three days' sail east, living quietly under a name that is not his own, deducible from a token cluster involving an old church registry, a harbour pilot's description, and a scar mentioned in a letter.

When all three chains are active, the trade becomes possible: Sera acquires Castillo's information to trade to Van der Hoek for the fragment.

**The branch point**: Sera could sell the chart fragment in Basin Two instead of trading it. This path is available. It closes Basin Three permanently and opens a short, quiet resolution: she has money and no answers. The game ends early with an acknowledgement that this was a valid choice that led somewhere smaller.

What Castillo's information actually contains — the SECRET token that surfaces here — recontextualises everything: Álvaro Dias did not bury the cache voluntarily. He buried it because he was being followed, and the organisation following him in 1541 has been maintaining that interest continuously for a hundred and fifty-six years. The archive has never been unguarded.

---

## Basin Three — Reach The Island

The voyage takes twelve days in good wind. The basin's obstacle tokens are environmental and navigational — three hazards on the approach route that the chart's coordinates do not fully account for because Dias surveyed them in different season conditions.

Castillo knows the seasonal corrections. He is not on the ship. His knowledge is in a document he gave Sera that she has not yet fully read because it is in Portuguese and her Portuguese is adequate but not fluent.

This basin uses the ABILITY token class most heavily. Mole the carpenter can read Portuguese better than Sera — he spent four years in Lisbon. But Mole and Sera have a tension encoded in the crew's EMOTION tokens from Basin One that means she has not told him what the document contains. The basin requires her to tell him. The enabling token — a conversation token that can only be placed after three crew relationship tokens are active — is the basin's emotional pivot. When it surfaces the crew stops being a collection of individuals with separate agendas.

The island is approached from the northwest. The eastern approach is blocked by a reef system that the chart marks but does not explain. The explanation is in Castillo's document. The reef is not natural.

---

## Basin Four — The Archive And What Guards It

The archive is guarded by a system, not a person — physical and procedural protections Dias built into the island in 1541, maintained by the organisation whose tokens have been accumulating since the SECRET surfaced in Basin Two. The organisation is Portuguese, ecclesiastical, and has been protecting the archive not to suppress its contents but to control when and how they are released. Their representative on the island is an elderly monk named Tomás who has been there for eleven years.

The basin opens when Sera can demonstrate three things to Tomás: legitimate connection to the archive through her father's work; that she understands what releasing the charts will actually mean for Pacific trade routes and has thought about it more carefully than the trading companies who want them; and that she has something to offer — not money, but the information in Castillo's document that the organisation has been seeking since 1671.

The final triad: Sera's father's original survey notes (which have been in her coat pocket since the game's opening and whose token has been on the casebook since turn two, significance unclear until now), Castillo's complete document, and the conversation with Tomás about what the charts are actually for.

When these three tokens are placed in proximity the field resolves. Not with violence. With a negotiation that requires everything the player has assembled — the characters, the knowledge, the trust — to be present simultaneously.

The cache opens. The archive is real. It is exactly what her father believed it was. What Sera does with it is the question the game ends on, without answering.

---

## Design notes

The player who pursues direct confrontation with Van der Hoek in Basin Two loses access to Castillo entirely — the confrontation makes Sera wanted in Nassau and the eastern island voyage becomes impossible. The player who assembles the crew too quickly in Basin One, without resolving the debt properly, loses the harbour master's cooperation and must approach the island from the south, which is longer and exposes the ship to the reef without the chart's full correction.

Adventure cases encode a different relation between player and field. In a mystery, the field pulls toward one fixed truth and the player's choices determine how they arrive. In an adventure, the player's choices determine what becomes possible. Branch points are not puzzles with one correct solution. They are decisions with consequences. The Hopfield field records them. The world arrived at reflects the choices made while getting there.
