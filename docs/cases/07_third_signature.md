The Aldgate Club — not the conservatory of Case 05, a different Aldgate — occupies a Georgian townhouse in Bloomsbury that smells permanently of pipe tobacco and old paper. Members sign in at the door. The sign-in book is kept by a porter named Wills who has been there since 1908 and remembers faces. The reading room is private after eight pm. Members may request the key from Wills. The key is logged.

On Wednesday evening a literary executor named Charles Pemberton — sixty-two, grey, careful — is found in the reading room at ten fifteen. He has been dead for approximately ninety minutes. The physician records cardiac arrest. He had a history of heart irregularities. He had been under stress.

---

## The Manuscript

In 1921 a novel was published under the name of Julian Marsh, a writer of some reputation who died in 1929. The novel — called The Sulphur and the Stone — was his last and is considered by critics his finest. It was published posthumously, appearing eighteen months after his death, edited and submitted by his literary estate.

Pemberton is the estate's executor. He has been since 1930.

The novel was not written by Julian Marsh. Marsh was ill from 1920 onward and produced no sustained work after 1919. The novel was written by a woman then known as Agnes Rutherford, who had been Marsh's secretary and companion from 1918 until his death. She wrote it between 1920 and 1922, showed it to no one, and eventually gave it to Marsh's estate after his death because she did not know what else to do with it and because she believed, perhaps correctly, that a novel submitted under her own name in 1929 would not have found a publisher.

Pemberton discovered the truth in 1929. He and Agnes — now Agnes Vail, married to a retired diplomat — reached an arrangement. She receives a percentage of royalties paid to the Marsh estate. She is not credited. She does not exist in the official record of the work. This has continued for six years.

---

## The Crisis of Conscience

Pemberton has decided to come clean. Not from moral courage — from fear. A researcher at the British Library has been working on a Marsh biography for two years and has recently requested access to correspondence from the 1918–1922 period. Pemberton has been managing this request with delays and misdirections. He cannot manage it indefinitely. The correspondence, if released, contains letters from Agnes that describe the novel's composition in her hand.

He met Agnes on Tuesday and told her he intended to file a correction with the publisher and cooperate with the biographer. He offered to share the legal burden. He presented this as the only viable option. Agnes agreed. She did not agree.

---

## Agnes

She is sixty-seven. She lives in Kensington in a quiet comfort that the Marsh estate royalties have made possible. She has a reputation as an occasional essayist, a dinner guest of quality, a woman of culture and discretion. She has a past that she has managed for forty years with extraordinary care.

The past is this: in 1921, before the novel was completed, Agnes was involved in a financial arrangement that later became a scandal. Not her arrangement — her husband's. But she was a signatory. If her real identity is connected to the manuscript — if her name appears in the literary record in connection with 1921 — a researcher will eventually connect Agnes Rutherford to the scandal. The connection will not destroy her. But it will end the quiet life she has built. At sixty-seven she has decided that is not acceptable.

She signed the club register at eight-five. The port on Pemberton's table came from a decanter she had access to for seven minutes while Wills was dealing with another member at the door. The compound — tasteless, untraceable in 1935 — was in her evening bag. Its effects are not immediate; Pemberton died approximately ninety minutes after Agnes left the building, which is consistent with the compound's action timeline and with his existing cardiac history.

She signed out at eight forty. Pemberton was found at ten fifteen.

---

## The Rival Estate

The literary estate of a contemporary of Marsh's has been contesting the attribution of The Sulphur and the Stone through legal channels for two years. They believe the novel contains passages derivative of their own client's unpublished work. Their solicitor, a man named Carey, was at the club on Wednesday evening for a dinner entirely unrelated to the Marsh estate. He is placed near the reading room corridor by two witnesses at eight-thirty. He has documented professional antagonism toward Pemberton.

He is the false plateau. Players who follow Carey build a coherent picture of professional dispute, legal maneuvering, and financial motivation that satisfies `killer_dim` and parts of `motive_dim` but cannot resolve `mechanism_dim` — because the mechanism requires the compound, and the compound requires access to the port, and Carey had no access to the port and no reason to have any.

The break comes through the sign-in log. Agnes's eight-five entry, cross-referenced with the key log for the reading room, shows she was in the building when Pemberton arrived. The porter Wills remembers her. He remembers the seven minutes at the door. He has said nothing because no one has asked him the right question.

The right question is a LATE phase witness token that surfaces only after the mechanism cluster is substantially assembled.

## Design notes

The compound's ninety-minute delay is mechanically important. Agnes was out of the building before Pemberton showed symptoms. The mechanism tokens must establish this timeline: the compound acts slowly, its cardiac presentation matches his existing history, the ninety minutes between eight-fifteen and ten-fifteen accounts for the delay without requiring Agnes's presence. The TIME tokens in the mechanism cluster are what close this dimension.

The 1921 scandal is the buried motive. The surface motive — protect the manuscript attribution — is visible by turn seven or eight. The buried motive (the scandal connection, the signatory document, the specific threat that Pemberton's disclosure creates) is what `motive:1921_scandal` carries. The LATE phase motive tokens build this: the 1921 date, the financial arrangement, the question of what Agnes's husband was involved in that required her signature.

Wills the porter is the case's most important witness token. He has been at the club since 1908, remembers every face, noticed the seven-minute gap at the door, and has said nothing because no one has asked him the right way. The witness token should be high temperature — his testimony is specific but he has no interpretation of what he saw. He offers fact; the player provides meaning.

The novel's title — The Sulphur and the Stone — echoes Case 08 (The Sulphur Line). This is not a narrative connection. It is the world's way of suggesting that sulphur, as an element of Victorian industrial crime, saturates more than one story.
