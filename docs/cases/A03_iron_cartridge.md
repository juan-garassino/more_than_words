# Adventure Case A03 — The Iron Cartridge
## American Frontier · 1876 · The Year After Little Bighorn

| | |
|---|---|

| **Type** | Adventure (sequential basins, chosen state) |
| **Difficulty** | medium |
| **Basins** | 4 |
| **Protagonist** | Elias Drum, 31, interpreter and surveyor's assistant |

*The army is looking for someone to blame and finding plenty of candidates.*

---

Elias Drum is thirty-one, mixed-race, the son of a Lakota woman and a former army scout who died at the Rosebud two months ago. He is fluent in four languages, has worked as an interpreter, a surveyor's assistant, and briefly as a journalist, and currently possesses a document his father gave him three days before the Rosebud — a signed affidavit describing events at a gold mining operation in the Black Hills in 1873 that involves officers still in active service, civilian contractors still operating, and a treaty violation that was systematic rather than incidental.

His father told him to get the document to a newspaper editor in Chicago named Ida Crane. She has been building this story for two years. The affidavit is the last piece she needs.

Between Elias and Chicago are eight hundred miles of territory in which he is legally ambiguous, politically inconvenient, and being followed by at least two parties whose interest in the document is not journalistic.

---

## Basin One — Get Out Of The Territory

A small settlement in Dakota Territory. A horse developing a limp. Two men who have been watching the settlement's only hotel since yesterday morning.

Three conditions before he can move: transport that will hold for eight hundred miles, a route avoiding the army checkpoints that have proliferated since Little Bighorn, and a way to leave without the watchers following him immediately.

The transport solution requires four CHARACTER tokens: a Mennonite community twenty miles south who are also trying to move east and have two wagons, a wagon that can carry a horse with a developing limp, and a community that will not answer questions from army officers on theological grounds. Getting to the Mennonites requires addressing the limp first — the blacksmith has the knowledge, the materials are in the trading post, and the trading post owner wants something Elias has that is not the document.

The two watchers are THREAT tokens. A token chain involving their equipment, their accents, and a company mark on their saddlebags reveals they are not military — they are civilian contractors named in the affidavit. This is a MID phase SECRET token that recontextualises their threat level: they are not authorised to act in a settlement, only to follow and report. The window to leave without consequence is before their report reaches anyone who can authorise more.

---

## Basin Two — The River Crossing

The Missouri River in October. Ferry crossings are logged. Army patrols check the logs. Elias under his own name will be flagged within twelve hours of crossing at any official point.

The solution is identity: a postal rider who makes irregular crossings for private clients, a woman named Ruth Mackay who runs a river trading operation and knew Elias's father and will vouch for him under a different name, and three OBJECT tokens — a different hat, different papers, a letter of introduction in technical lettering his surveyor's training can produce.

Ruth Mackay is the basin's most important CHARACTER token. She has her own agenda: she is also trying to move a document east, concerning land title fraud affecting her trading route. Her document and Elias's concern different crimes committed by overlapping sets of people.

**The key branch**: the player who assembles enough of her token cluster realises this and must decide whether to tell her. Telling her doubles the political weight of what they are both carrying and makes both of them more important to stop. Not telling her leaves her exposed to consequences she doesn't know are coming.

The basin closes when they are across the river and the identity has held.

---

## Basin Three — Minneapolis And What Waits There

Ida Crane's contact in Minneapolis — a printer named Josiah Webb — has been arrested for an unrelated debt. He is in the county jail. The secondary evidence for the story is in his padlocked print shop.

The token chain is urban and legal. A local abolitionist network has relationships with the county court system. A lawyer can address the debt claim given specific information about the creditor — assembable from three LOCATION tokens in the commercial district. The padlock's manufacture matches the sheriff's deputy's personal tool set rather than the official county supply: a MODIFIER token that suggests the padlock was placed as a favour rather than officially, which means it can be removed as a favour in return.

When the secondary evidence is retrieved, it contains something Ida Crane does not know is there: a list of names. The list includes a United States Senator on the committee that oversees Indian Affairs. This LATE phase SECRET token explains why the contractors have been authorised to follow Elias across three territories rather than waiting for the document to surface through normal channels.

---

## Basin Four — Chicago And Ida Crane

The simplest structure. The most complex execution.

Three things stand between Elias and the story running. A legal injunction already prepared, waiting to be filed the moment the story's existence becomes officially known. The newspaper's owner — a man named Hartford, being pressured through his banking relationships to kill the story before it runs. And the contractors, who have been ahead of Elias since Minneapolis and are no longer merely watching.

The injunction is defeated through the Senator's list, filed through a legal channel that makes suppression more difficult than publication. Hartford is defeated through the secondary evidence plus a CHARACTER token from the Minneapolis network who has a relationship with Hartford's business partner. The contractors are defeated through a choice about visibility: Elias can approach the newspaper openly, which risks interception but creates witnesses, or through a back channel that is safer but requires trusting one more person not yet fully assembled in the token field.

The final triad: the affidavit, Ida Crane, and the morning edition.

---

## Design notes

The moral weight accumulates across basins. Telling Ruth about the overlapping documents in Basin Two changes the world of the story — it makes her a participant in something larger than she knew. Not telling her leaves her in a danger she cannot see. Neither choice is presented as correct. The field records it and continues.

The document is never described in detail. Its power is structural — what it represents to Elias, what it cost his father, what it will cost Ida Crane to publish. The affidavit token's surface expression is brief and factual: a date, a place, a name. The weight is in the field around it.

This is the most contemporary of the three adventure cases in its political geometry, but its shape is identical to the others: a protagonist moving through successive obstacles with accumulated knowledge, toward a final state that requires everything assembled to reach. The Hopfield field does not care what year it is. It cares about what the player has placed on the board.
