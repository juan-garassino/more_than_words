---

Aldous Verne is fifty-four years old and has been a financier for thirty years. He is not a good man but he is a precise one, and precision has finally caught up with him. On the evening of November 14th he boards a train at Liverpool Street carrying a leather satchel and a cipher telegram folded into the breast pocket of his coat. The telegram is addressed to the Board of Trade. He intends to deliver it in person the following morning.

He never reaches morning.

The telegram contains four years of systematic fraud. Not his fraud. His partner's. Renard Voss has been redirecting client funds through a subsidiary that exists only on paper, and the subsidiary connects — through three careful degrees of separation — to a man whose name appears in the House of Lords. Verne discovered this eight weeks ago. He said nothing. He spent six of those weeks deciding whether to act and two of them constructing the cipher. He told no one. He trusted no one. He was right not to.

What he did not know is that the solicitor who drew up the original partnership documents twenty years ago has a clerk. The clerk has debts. Renard Voss has patience.

---

## The Station

Thornfield Crossing is a junction station in the Essex marshes, unmanned after eleven pm. The platform lights work. The office lights do not. The last train south passes through at eleven twenty. The first train north arrives at six fifteen. Between those two times the station belongs to the fog.

The stationmaster, Hoole, has worked this junction for nineteen years and has never done anything wrong in his life until the evening of November 14th, when he accepts an envelope from a man he has never met and agrees to unlock the station office at eleven fifteen and be elsewhere until morning. He tells himself the envelope contains money owed for a service rendered. He does not open it that night. When he does open it — two days later, in his kitchen, with his wife asleep upstairs — he finds it contains exactly what he feared and burns it in the grate.

## The Daughter

Clara Verne is thirty-one. She and her father have not spoken in four years, since her marriage to a man he considered beneath the family. The marriage ended eighteen months ago. She has been composing a letter of reconciliation for six months and has never sent it. On November 14th she decides not to send a letter. She takes the train instead.

She arrives at Thornfield Crossing on the nine forty-seven, two hours before her father. She waits on platform two. She watches the last train arrive and sees her father step onto the platform with his satchel and his measured walk. She starts toward him across the footbridge. She is halfway across when she sees another man step out of the station office doorway. She does not recognise him. She does not think much of it — passengers sometimes wait inside when the platform is cold.

She reaches platform one thirty seconds after her father disappears into the fog at the northern end. She follows. She finds him four minutes later. She does not scream. She kneels. She checks for breath. There is none. She stays there for forty minutes, unable to move, and then walks to the nearest farmhouse and asks them to telephone the police.

When the police arrive she tells them about the man in the station office doorway. She describes his coat, his height, the angle of his hat. She does not know his name. She has never seen him before. She will see him once more, at the inquest, where he appears as a character witness for the stationmaster.

The police do not connect these things. The player must.

---

## The Satchel

Renard Voss arrives on the last train with his own satchel — dark leather, brass fittings, initialed R.V. in a stamp that was a gift from his first wife twenty years ago. He brings it because he intends to take the telegram and leave. He takes the telegram. In the dark and the fog and the speed of what he has just done he picks up the wrong satchel. Aldous Verne's bag is almost identical. Voss's satchel — initialed, distinctive, irreplaceable — is left on the platform beside the body.

This is the only mistake he makes. It is sufficient.

## The Three Paths

A player who begins with Clara — with her grief, her silence, her description of the man in the doorway — builds a field that warms slowly toward Voss. The emotion tokens activate early. The witness tokens surface mid-game. The stationmaster's evasion appears late and redirects everything.

A player who begins with the satchel — with the initials R.V., with the fact that the victim's name is Aldous Verne and these are not his initials — arrives faster. The object cluster pulls hard toward Voss from the third turn. But this path skips the daughter entirely and arrives at the truth without understanding the human geometry around it.

A player who begins with the stationmaster (Hoole) — his evasion, his presence, his unlocked office — follows the longest path. He looks guilty for eight turns. The enabler token, when it surfaces, is the envelope: not found, burned, but described by his wife who noticed the smell of paper smoke on a night when there was no fire lit. This token bridges Hoole's apparent guilt into Voss's actual guilt and breaks the false plateau.

Every path ends at the same three tokens. The satchel. The telegram. Renard Voss.

## Design notes

The opening triad establishes the station at night: fog, platform, body. This is the OPENING stream — fixed scene-setting tokens that give the field its initial energy.

The stationmaster is the case's most effective red herring because his guilt is real in a limited sense: he took the money, enabled the access, said nothing. The player who pursues him is not wrong. They are following a genuine thread to a node that points toward the true killer rather than being the true killer.

Clara's path is the most emotionally complete. It is the slowest path to the invariants and the one that best illuminates why the crime happened when it did. The reconciliation letter she never sent is a mid-game EMOTION token that has no mechanical bearing on the solution but significantly raises the temperature of the field.

The clerk Croft — the solicitor's assistant who sent the note — is a LATE phase token. His surface expression is indirect. He never appears in person. He is the information channel through which Voss learned about the telegram, and placing him is the moment `motive_dim` begins to move.
