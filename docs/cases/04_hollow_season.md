---

Ashworth Park is the kind of house that requires twelve staff to maintain and generates enough income to employ nine. The gap is covered by the estate's timber rights, which expire in 1912, and by Sir Geoffrey Carrow's careful management, which will not survive him. His nephew Edmund knows this. His solicitor knows this. The new will, drafted on Thursday and not yet witnessed by a second party by Sunday morning, knows this in the most binding sense.

Sir Geoffrey is found in the library before breakfast on Sunday. The french windows are open. The morning is cold. He has a known chest complaint. He takes a nightly brandy. He is sixty-three years old and has been declining gently for two years in a way that makes the discovery sad but not, in most minds, suspicious.

In most minds.

---

## Edmund

Edmund Carrow is thirty-eight and has been waiting for this inheritance since his mother — Sir Geoffrey's sister — died when Edmund was twelve and Geoffrey became his guardian. He is not a cruel man. He is a frightened one. The estate is not just money to Edmund. It is the house his mother was born in, the garden she described to him on her deathbed, the specific smell of the library in August that he associates with the only period of his childhood that felt stable. He has never said any of this to anyone.

He learned about the will change on Friday evening. The solicitor's assistant — a young man named Croft who owes Edmund a sum he cannot repay — sent a note that arrived at Edmund's rooms in the village at six forty-five pm. Edmund burned the note. He came to the house for dinner as previously arranged, behaved normally through the evening, and went to bed at eleven.

At some point between eleven pm and six am he added a preparation to the brandy decanter in the library. The preparation is a sleeping compound available without prescription at any chemist, used in small doses for insomnia. In a larger dose, administered to a man with a chest complaint who then sleeps near an open window on a cold August night, its effects are more final.

Edmund opened the window himself. He came down at three am. He left it open. He went back to bed. He was the first person to suggest, on Sunday morning, that his uncle's chest complaint had finally taken him.

---

## The Estate Manager

Hector Finch has managed the Ashworth Park estate for eleven years. For three of those years he has been skimming the timber accounts — not dramatically, not greedily, just enough to cover a debt he incurred before he came to this posting and has never been able to retire. He knows that Sir Geoffrey ordered an audit of the estate accounts two weeks ago. He knows that the audit was scheduled to begin on Monday. He knows what the audit will find.

He was in the house all weekend. He had access to the library. He had clearer and more immediate motive than Edmund — the audit would have ended not just his employment but his freedom.

He is the red herring the game sustains longest because his guilt, while false, is entirely coherent. Players who follow Finch will build a complete and internally consistent explanation for the death that satisfies `killer_dim` and `mechanism_dim` before `motive_dim` forces them elsewhere.

The motive tokens that break the Finch plateau are the ones that carry Edmund's emotional geography — the house, the mother, the specific quality of his fear. These tokens are the hardest to write and the most important to get right. They are LATE phase, high temperature, and their surface expressions describe observed behaviour: Edmund's habit of touching the library doorframe when he enters, the way he stood at his mother's portrait for eleven minutes before dinner on Saturday, the expression that crossed his face when Finch mentioned the audit at breakfast. None of these things are evidence. Together they are a portrait of a man who could not afford to lose the house.

---

## The New Will

The will is the pivot. The player who finds it early — through the solicitor token, through the Thursday date, through the witness token that places Croft at the postal box on Friday evening — has the case's structural spine before turn six. But having the spine does not mean having the case. The spine explains what Edmund stood to lose. The sleeping compound, the window, the brandy decanter are the mechanism tokens that take another six to eight turns to fully assemble.

The false plateau occurs when players establish the will change and Edmund's knowledge of it but cannot connect him physically to the mechanism. The bridging token is the chemist's receipt — not found in Edmund's possession but in the wastepaper basket of his rooms in the village, collected by the housekeeper who was saving paper for reuse and has not yet emptied the basket when the player investigates. Its surface expression is unremarkable: a date, a preparation, a small sum. Its placement adjacent to the brandy decanter token shifts the field immediately.

---

## Design notes

The distinction between Edmund's motive and Finch's motive is the case's central design problem. Both motives are economically grounded — inheritance vs audit exposure. But Edmund's motive has an emotional layer (the house as the site of his only stable memory) that Finch's does not. The LATE phase emotional tokens carry this layer. They are high temperature because grief and attachment are ambiguous — they don't read directly as motive from the outside.

The window is the mechanism's understated detail. The compound alone might not have killed a healthier man. The combination — sedated sleep in cold air, chest complaint, open window — is what makes it both lethal and deniable. The mechanism cluster should make this combination visible: sleeping compound token + chest complaint token + open window token together establish causation, while any two alone are insufficient.

Croft the solicitor's assistant appears in both this case and Case 01 (amber cipher) as a character name. In Case 01, Croft is a different person — a young solicitor's clerk in Voss's circle. The name recurs as a small detail of the world, not a canonical connection.

The audit token is one of the most functional red herring tokens in the set. It actively draws players toward Finch for five to seven turns before the will change surfaces and redirects. The audit and the will change are simultaneous pressures — both real, both operating over the same weekend — which is why Finch looks so credible for so long.
