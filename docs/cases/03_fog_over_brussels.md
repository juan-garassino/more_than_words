# Case 03 — Fog Over Brussels
## Belgian Embassy · Winter · 1961

*Cross-case note: The compound used here is the same family of synthetic cardiac accelerant that appears in Case 07 (The Third Signature). Neither case acknowledges the other within the fiction — they are separated by twenty-four years and two countries — but the world contains one compound and it has been used twice.*

---

The embassy occupies a corner building on the Rue de la Loi that was requisitioned in 1946 and has never felt entirely like it belongs to anyone. The heating works in the east wing only. The cipher room is in the west wing. This detail matters.

It is December 4th, 1961. There is a diplomatic reception on the ground floor. Sixty-three guests. Four languages. Two dozen conversations that the guest list suggests were accidental and the seating plan confirms were deliberate. At ten forty-seven pm a cipher clerk named Ilse Brenner is found in the document room adjacent to the cipher room. The attending physician records cardiac arrest. She is twenty-nine years old.

---

## Ilse

Ilse Brenner has worked in the embassy cipher room for three years. She is precise, quiet, and has a gift for pattern recognition that her supervisor has noted twice in her file and never acted on. On November 28th she decoded a routine diplomatic communication and found something inside it that was not routine. A secondary encoding — not hidden, exactly, but layered in a way that only someone looking for it would find, and only someone with her specific pattern recognition would be looking.

The secondary encoding named a source. The source was inside the embassy. The source had been active for four years.

She spent four days deciding what to do. On December 2nd she began drafting a report. On December 3rd she told no one but left the draft in a folder on her desk rather than in the locked drawer where she kept sensitive documents. On December 4th she attended the reception because attendance was mandatory for all staff. She accepted a glass of champagne from a passing tray at eight-fifteen pm. She was found at ten forty-seven.

---

## Brennan

The cultural attaché is forty-four, Irish-born, and has worked in diplomatic postings across four countries since 1949. He is charming in the way that only people who have practiced charm as a professional skill can be — which is to say entirely convincingly until the moment it isn't. He has been the embassy's secondary source for Soviet intelligence since 1957. He did not choose this role in 1957. He was placed in a position in 1955 where choosing it was the only option that preserved something he valued more than his integrity. What that something was the player discovers only through the motive tokens.

He administered the compound in the champagne glass. Not his hand — the tray was carried by a catering staff member who does not know what he carried. Brennan intercepted the tray in the corridor between the kitchen and the reception room for forty seconds. The compound is a synthetic cardiac accelerant developed for field use. It is tasteless. It produces no marks. In 1961 it does not appear on any standard post-mortem panel.

He knew about Ilse's draft report because he had a copy made of her desk key eighteen months earlier as a standard precaution.

---

## The Ambassador's Wife

Helena Voss — no relation to Renard Voss of the amber cipher — is fifty-two and has lived inside diplomatic protocol for so long that deviation from it registers to her as physical discomfort. She and Ilse argued on November 29th about a matter that appears in the witness tokens as a disagreement about document filing procedure and is actually about something more personal that the player pieces together across four mid-game tokens. Helena had been using the cipher room's secondary terminal to send personal communications that bypassed the official log. Ilse had noticed. They had an arrangement: silence for silence. The arrangement broke down on November 29th.

Helena has motive. Helena has no alibi for the relevant window — she left the reception at eight-ten and returned at eight-fifty. Helena has been seen by two witnesses in a state of agitation near the document room corridor. Helena is not guilty.

The path through Helena is the longest in the game and the most emotionally resonant. It ends not at Helena's guilt but at her knowledge — she saw Brennan intercept the catering tray. She did not understand what she saw. She has said nothing because saying anything requires explaining why she was in that corridor at that time and what she was doing there.

The late-game witness token that carries this information is one of the highest-weight tokens in the case. When it surfaces the `killer_dim` dimension saturates almost immediately.

---

## The Compound

The mechanism tokens in this case form the tightest cluster. The compound, the tray, the forty-second window, the cardiac presentation, the post-mortem panel that cannot detect it — these five tokens are densely connected. They are all MID or LATE phase. They do not surface until the player has established the basic geography of the reception.

The most important mechanism token is a TIME token: the forty-second interval between the tray leaving the kitchen and arriving at the reception room. This is longer than it should be for the distance involved. The catering supervisor noticed it at the time and did not think it significant. The player who places this token adjacent to the compound token on the casebook will feel the field shift. The energy drops sharply. The oracle surfaces the Brennan token within two turns.

---

## Design notes

This case has the most compressed time window of any in the set. Everything happens between 8:05 and 8:55 pm. The fog of the reception — sixty-three guests, four languages, constant movement — is what makes the window possible. The mechanism tokens map this geography: kitchen, corridor, reception room, tray, forty seconds.

The difficulty rating is hard because both major false leads are structurally complete in isolation. Helena's path satisfies `killer_dim`, `mechanism_dim` partially, and advances `motive_dim` to about 0.45. Brennan's surface presentation is that of a charming, uninvolved peripheral figure — his guilt is buried behind the most normal behaviour in the room.

Helena's testimony token is the hardest to place because it requires the player to ask the right question of a character who does not volunteer the information. The witness token should surface only after the compound cluster is substantially assembled — the player needs to be asking "how was it delivered" before they can receive "here is who delivered it."

Brennan's motive — what he was protecting in 1955 — is deliberately unspecified. The motive tokens name the structure (the arrangement, the leverage, the four years of compliance) without naming its content. The `motive:double_agent_exposure` token carries the fact, not the feeling. The feeling is left to the player.
